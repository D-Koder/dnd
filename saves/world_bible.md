# World Bible

*This file is the persistent memory of the world. Update it as new places/factions/NPCs matter.*

## The Setting
Veyrune — old magic and crashed spaceship tech mashed together a few centuries ago.

## Currency
The standard currency is the **coil** — small stamped discs of copper, silver, and gold, re-minted from salvaged old-world wiring and valued partly for their conductivity as much as their worth. A hot meal runs 1-2 copper coils; a night's lodging around 1 silver coil; passage on a coastal trade ship 5-10 silver coils. Old-tech relics are often bartered directly rather than sold for coin, since their value is hard to standardize.

## Settlements
- **Port Halloway** (town) — A scrappy trade hub built around the crashed hull of a sky-ship. Bar, market, guild board. Confirmed to be one piece of a deliberately scattered fleet — partial designation "...N-3."
- **Bellcaster's Reach** (village) — A fishing and salvage-diving village on the same coastline as Port Halloway. Known for divers who work the shallow wrecks offshore.
- **Ironspire** (kingdom) — Capital of the Ashgrave Kingdom, the nearest major political power. A walled inland city, partly built from scavenged old-tech scaffolding.
- **The Rustlands** (outpost) — A stretch of open water further out past Bellcaster's Reach, littered with wreckage from the scattered fleet. Dangerous, sparsely charted — rumored to hold more Anchor sites.

## Known Locations

## Rules of This World
- "Magic" and "tech" are the same thing, just different vocabulary — old-world scientists and old-world wizards were arguing about the same stuff.
- Nothing here needs to be taken too seriously. If it's funnier the weird way, go with funnier.

### How Combat Works
- Attack roll = d20 + grounding modifier, vs a target number the DM sets (typically 10-15).
- Hit damage = roll a d6.
- Grounding modifiers: Wet Earth +3, On Water/On Stone +2, Ungrounded -2.

### How Stats Work
- Six ability scores, real D&D 5e math: **Strength** (force/toughness), **Dexterity** (precision/reflexes), **Constitution** (endurance, and determines HP gained per level), **Intelligence** (tech/relics/puzzles), **Wisdom** (perception/instinct/composure), **Charisma** (social/presence/scheming).
- Modifier = floor((score-10)/2) — a score of 10-11 is +0, every 2 points above/below shifts the modifier by 1. Scores cap at 20.
- A non-magic roll can specify which ability applies; the app adds that ability's modifier *plus* the character's current proficiency bonus automatically. Magic/grounding rolls are unaffected and keep using the existing water/stone/wet mechanic.
- Raken's starting array (15/14/13/12/10/8 assigned by concept): INT 15, WIS 14, DEX 13, CHA 12, CON 10, STR 8 — a careful tinkerer who isn't built for a fight.

### How Leveling Works
- Real D&D 5e XP thresholds — cumulative total, never resets: 300 XP reaches level 2, 900 reaches level 3, 2700 for level 4, climbing steeply from there up to level 20.
- Proficiency bonus scales with level: +2 at levels 1-4, +3 at 5-8, +4 at 9-12, +5 at 13-16, +6 at 17-20.
- Each level grants max HP equal to the average roll of the character's hit die (rounded up) plus their Constitution modifier — Raken's hit die is d8, so roughly +5 HP per level before the Constitution adjustment — plus a full heal and a banked skill point. Every even level also grants +1 max Magic Reserve.
- Ability Score Improvements happen only at levels 4, 8, 12, 16, and 19 (the standard progression), granting 2 points total to freely allocate — both into one score, or split across two, same as the real rule's "+2 one score or +1/+1 two scores" choice.

### How Attacks & Skills Work
- **Attacks** are fixed reference moves — each has a type (melee/ranged/magic), an associated ability score, a damage die, a damage type, and sometimes a Magic Reserve cost. Damage on a hit = the die roll + that ability's modifier. A resource-costing attack spends its cost whether the attack hits or not.
- **Skills** are named capabilities (Relic Read, Scheme, Ground & Channel, Jury-Rig for Raken) that are binary — proficient or not, same as real D&D, no ranks or point-buy. Being proficient adds the character's current proficiency bonus to a roll using that skill; untrained adds nothing beyond the raw ability modifier.
- New skill proficiency choices are granted at levels 4, 8, 12, 16, and 19, alongside the Ability Score Improvement at those same levels — a homebrew adaptation, since there's no class/background here to hand out fixed proficiencies the normal way.
- **Status effects** (fire, poison, bleed, etc.) on an enemy are tracked for display only — the DM still narrates and applies the ongoing damage each turn manually, the tracker just keeps it visible so it isn't forgotten.

### How Equipment Slots Work
- Real slots, not an unlimited list: Head, Body, Feet, and two Accessory slots each hold exactly one item — equipping something new to an occupied slot swaps the old item back to inventory automatically.
- Hands have a real 2-slot capacity: a one-handed item (most weapons, tools) costs 1, a two-handed item costs both. Can't equip beyond capacity — the app refuses it and explains why, rather than silently allowing it.
- Extra hand capacity beyond the normal 2 only comes from a specific in-story ability (an extra limb, a summoned appendage, etc) — never assumed by default.

### How Getting Stronger Actually Works
- Ability Score Improvements and rising Hit Points are real, but they're a slow trickle — most of a D&D character's power growth actually comes from unlocking new things to do, not bigger numbers on old things (a Fighter's Extra Attack, a Wizard's new spell levels, etc). There's no class system here, so Raken has a small hand-built table of level-gated unlocks instead: a new attack at level 3, an upgraded Improvised Strike at 5, a new skill at 7, an upgraded Focus Pulse at 10.
- Proficiency bonus climbing with level (+2 up to +6) is also a major, often-underrated source of growing power — it applies to every proficient skill, save, and attack at once.

### How Defenses Work
- **Armor Class (AC)** = 10 + Dexterity modifier (plus any armor bonus). This is the number an attacker's d20 roll needs to meet or beat to land a hit.
- **Passive Perception** = 10 + Wisdom modifier. The DM uses this to decide what the character notices without calling for an active roll.
- **Saving throws** are their own category, separate from skill checks — each of the 6 abilities has its own save, and the character can be proficient in specific ones (Raken: Intelligence and Wisdom, matching a real Wizard's save proficiencies). Proficiency bonus applies only if proficient in that specific save.
- **Advantage/Disadvantage**: certain rolls are made with advantage (roll 2d20, take the higher) or disadvantage (take the lower) instead of a flat single roll — reflects real situational edges or hindrances, not a permanent bonus.

### How Death Saves Work
- At 0 HP, the character isn't just "unconscious" — real death saves apply. Roll a d20: 10 or higher is a success, under 10 is a failure. A natural 20 revives outright at 1 HP. A natural 1 counts as two failures. Three successes stabilizes (still at 0 HP, no longer actively dying); three failures ends it — respecting the Hardcore Mode setting either way.
- Any real healing above 0 HP clears an ongoing death save count entirely.

### How Conditions Work
- Standard D&D conditions (Blinded, Charmed, Frightened, Grappled, Paralyzed, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, Unconscious, etc.) can be applied and removed by the DM as the story calls for them — see the Library tab's Conditions reference for what each one actually does mechanically.
- **Exhaustion** is tracked separately as a 0-6 level rather than a simple on/off condition, since its severity escalates (disadvantage on checks, halved speed, disadvantage on attacks/saves, halved max HP, and so on up to level 6, which is fatal).

### How Resting Works
- **Short Rest** recovers some HP (average hit-die roll + Constitution modifier) and about half of max Magic Reserve.
- **Long Rest** fully restores HP and Magic Reserve, clears any death save progress, and reduces Exhaustion by 1 level.

### How Quests Work
- **Main Quest** — the throughline; 1-2 active at a time, never more. Drives earning coin, story loot, encounters, NPCs, and exploring/traveling the map. Never capacity-limited, never evicted.
- **Side Quests** — max 5 active. Quick-to-medium arcs giving real backstory/texture (an NPC's life, a local trouble). Rewards: simple items, reasonable coin, occasionally something rare.
- **Small Requests** — max 10 active. Lightweight discrete facts/favors/leads (a favor asked in passing, a rumor, a code, a deadline) — exist so small details survive many turns without being forgotten.
- **Unique Events** — no cap, but rare by design. Simpler in scope than a Main Quest but genuinely harder to pull off. Big payoff: epic loot, meaningful coin, and something that eases the main story.
- **Every quest has real structure**: up to 3 concrete goals (checkable steps, ticked off as they're actually accomplished, not vague vibes) and a reward that's never left blank. Reward conventions: "??" means not yet decided/revealed; a "!!" prefix flags something notably advantageous (a rare tool, useful info, a real edge — not just a generic prize); otherwise it's a plain description, and can list multiple things at once (coin + an item) when warranted. Reward size/nature scales to the quest's tier *and* the personality/means of whoever's offering it. Once a Main Quest's 3 goals are all done, it completes and the next one in the throughline takes over.
- **Story-connected but not the throughline itself** — something that supports or ties into the main plot without being the singular ongoing quest — is a Side Quest, not an extra Main Quest slot.
- **Pinning** — up to 2 Side Quests and 2 Small Requests can be pinned to exempt them from eviction (Main/Unique never need pinning, they're never evicted).
- **When Side/Small hits its cap**, a new one doesn't silently bump an old one — you get an Accept/Reject prompt showing exactly which (oldest, unpinned) quest would be dropped. Accepting evicts it with a "you've forgotten about..." note; rejecting just declines the new one.
- Actual current quests (with their goals/reward) live in session_log.md → Active Quests, not here — this section is just the rules of how the system works.
